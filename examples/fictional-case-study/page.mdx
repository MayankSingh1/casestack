## TL;DR

Acme Commerce's checkout flow was losing $18M annually to abandonment due to a fragmented 5-step process with hidden trust signals. I led a 4-month redesign that consolidated it to 2 steps, added smart defaults for international users, and surfaced trust badges at decision points. **Result: 23% higher completion rates and $4.2M in recovered annual revenue.**

## Context & Business Problem

Acme Commerce processes $2B in annual transactions across 40 countries. Despite strong brand recognition, **conversion from cart to purchase lagged competitors by 15 percentage points**. Checkout abandonment cost the company an estimated $18M annually.

The existing checkout flow was built 4 years prior for US-only customers. As Acme expanded internationally, the flow accumulated patches without a coherent redesign. Trust signals were buried, address formats confused international users, and forced account creation drove away one-time buyers.

Leadership prioritized checkout optimization as a Q2 initiative after customer research revealed trust and complexity as top friction points.

## My Role & Collaboration

As **Lead Product Designer**, I:

- Owned end-to-end checkout UX across web and mobile
- Led a team of 2 designers, conducting weekly design critiques
- Partnered with Data Science to define success metrics
- Facilitated stakeholder alignment across 3 business units
- Collaborated daily with engineering on feasibility trade-offs

I worked embedded with the Checkout team (1 PM, 5 engineers, 1 researcher) and coordinated with the International Growth team for localization requirements.

## Constraints

- **4-month deadline** for holiday shopping peak
- **Legacy payment APIs** requiring backward compatibility with 3 providers
- **12-language localization** with no dedicated team
- **No dedicated researcher**—I conducted guerrilla testing myself
- **Mobile-critical**: 62% of traffic, but 40% of revenue (conversion gap)

## Process & Key Decisions

### Decision: Mobile-First Redesign

**Options considered:**
- Desktop-first with mobile adaptation
- Parity across devices from start
- **Mobile-first (chosen)**

**Trade-off:** Desktop would inherit some touch-optimized patterns.

**Rationale:** 62% of traffic was mobile, but conversion was 15pp lower than desktop. Fixing mobile had the highest ROI.

### Decision: Hybrid Checkout (Collapsible Sections)

**Options considered:**
- Single-page (all fields visible)
- Multi-step wizard (one section per screen)
- **Hybrid collapsible (chosen)**

**Trade-off:** +2 weeks engineering time for more complex interactions.

**Rationale:** User testing showed 40% of users scan all information, 60% step through. Hybrid served both without forcing a choice.

### Decision: Guest Checkout as Default

**Options considered:**
- Sign-in prompt first
- Side-by-side guest/account options
- **Guest as default (chosen)**

**Trade-off:** Potential reduction in account creation.

**Rationale:** 34% of abandoned carts cited forced account creation. Defaulting to guest removed friction; users could still opt in to save info after purchase.

## Solution Walkthrough

A streamlined 2-step checkout flow with smart defaults, prominent trust signals, and seamless international support.

### Trust Badges Above Fold

Security badges, payment icons, and money-back guarantee moved to fixed position visible on first load.

*Design decision: 67% of users previously abandoned at payment step—trust signals were hidden below fold*

### Smart Address Lookup

Postal code lookup that auto-fills city/state and reorders fields based on user's locale.

*Reduced address fields from 7 to 2 for most users; international users see locale-appropriate field order*

### Guest Checkout Default

No account required. Users can optionally save info with one checkbox after purchase.

*Addressed the #1 abandonment reason: 'didn't want to create an account'*

### Sticky Order Summary

Running total with shipping estimate visible at all times, updating dynamically as options change.

*Users wanted clarity on total cost at every step—eliminated end-of-flow surprises*

## Reflection & Learnings

### What I Learned

- **Localization should start day 1**, not be retrofitted—we burned 2 weeks fixing field ordering issues
- **Stakeholder alignment workshops saved 3 weeks** of rework—the upfront investment paid off
- **Guerrilla testing is better than no testing**—even 5-minute hallway tests caught major issues

### What I'd Do Differently

- **Involve engineering earlier** in ideation—we hit avoidable technical debt on the address API integration
- **Set up metrics tracking before launch**—we had a 2-week data gap while analytics was configured
