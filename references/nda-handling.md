# NDA Handling Guide

How to present confidential work in your portfolio while respecting legal obligations.

## Understanding NDAs

### What NDAs Typically Restrict

- Company name and branding
- Unreleased products or features
- Exact financial figures
- Proprietary user research data
- Internal strategic documents
- Competitive intelligence
- Client names (for agencies)

### What's Usually Permitted

- Your role and responsibilities
- General problem domain
- Your design process
- Relative outcomes (percentages, not absolutes)
- Skills and methods used
- Anonymized case studies (with approval)

### When in Doubt

1. **Re-read your NDA** — Look for specific restrictions
2. **Ask your employer** — Many will approve sanitized versions
3. **Err on the side of caution** — Your reputation > one case study

## Anonymization Strategies

### Company Names

**Instead of:**
> At Stripe, I redesigned the dashboard...

**Use:**
> At a leading fintech company, I redesigned the dashboard...

**Descriptors to use:**
- "A Fortune 500 retail company"
- "A Series B fintech startup"
- "A major healthcare platform"
- "A top-10 social media app"

### Product Names

**Instead of:**
> I designed Stripe Atlas...

**Use:**
> I designed a business incorporation product...

### Visual Assets

**For screenshots:**
- Blur or redact sensitive data (names, numbers, emails)
- Replace real content with placeholder text
- Create representative mockups that show the concept without real data
- Use wireframes instead of final designs

**For Figma exports:**
- Remove company logos
- Replace real copy with realistic alternatives
- Anonymize user photos or avatars

### Metrics

**Instead of:**
> Revenue increased by $4.2M

**Use:**
> Revenue increased by ~20%
> Revenue impact in the low millions

**Instead of:**
> DAU reached 12 million

**Use:**
> DAU grew by 35%

## Disclosure Language

### Standard NDA Disclosure

Add a note at the top of the case study:

> **Note:** Due to confidentiality, company name and specific metrics are anonymized. Relative outcomes and process are accurate.

### Partial Disclosure

If some things can be shared:

> **Note:** This project was completed at [Company]. Some details are anonymized per company policy, but all metrics are actual.

### Agency Work

> **Note:** This work was completed for [Agency Name] for an undisclosed client. Details are representative of the actual project.

## What You Can Always Show

### Your Process

Your thinking process belongs to you. You can describe:

- How you approached the problem
- Methods you used
- Frameworks you applied
- How you collaborated
- What you learned

### Generic Outcomes

Even under strict NDA:

- "Conversion improved significantly"
- "Launch was considered a success by leadership"
- "The approach was adopted by other teams"
- "The project won an internal design award"

### Skills Demonstrated

- Research methods used
- Tools and technologies
- Collaboration approaches
- Problem-solving techniques

## Getting Approval

### How to Ask

Email your manager or legal:

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm updating my design portfolio and would like to include work from [Project]. I've drafted an anonymized version that:
> - Uses [descriptor] instead of company name
> - Shows only [approved/blurred] visuals
> - Uses relative metrics (percentages, not absolutes)
>
> Could you review and let me know if this is acceptable? Happy to adjust as needed.
>
> Draft attached.

### What Companies Often Approve

- Process descriptions
- Relative metrics
- Blurred or recreated visuals
- General problem statements
- Your role and contributions

### What Companies Often Reject

- Unreleased features
- Competitive positioning info
- Exact revenue/cost figures
- Internal research verbatim
- Strategic roadmap details

---

## Alternative Approaches

### If You Can't Show the Work

**Option 1: Process-only case study**
Focus entirely on your process, decisions, and learnings. Skip the solution visuals.

**Option 2: Side project**
Create a conceptual project that demonstrates similar skills.

**Option 3: Pre-NDA work**
Use older work that's no longer under restriction.

**Option 4: Open source contributions**
Showcase design work on public projects.

### Making Conceptual Work Credible

If you create a side project:

- State clearly it's a conceptual/side project
- Base it on real research if possible
- Apply the same rigor as client work
- Don't claim it shipped or had impact

---

## Red Lines

Never do these, regardless of how you anonymize:

❌ **Don't share unreleased features** — Even anonymized, timing can reveal
❌ **Don't share internal research verbatim** — Paraphrase findings instead
❌ **Don't share competitive intelligence** — Even old analysis
❌ **Don't share proprietary algorithms/logic** — Describe outcomes, not how
❌ **Don't lie** — Don't claim something shipped when it didn't

---

## Case Study NDA Checklist

Before publishing, verify:

- [ ] Company name anonymized (if required)
- [ ] Product name anonymized (if required)
- [ ] Screenshots redacted or recreated
- [ ] Metrics converted to relative (if required)
- [ ] No unreleased features shown
- [ ] No internal research quoted verbatim
- [ ] Disclosure note included
- [ ] Employer approval obtained (if required by NDA)

## Sample Disclosure Templates

### Full Anonymization

```
**Confidentiality Note**

This case study describes work completed at a [descriptor] company. Per NDA, 
company name, product details, and specific metrics are anonymized. The 
process, approach, and relative outcomes described are accurate.
```

### Partial Anonymization

```
**Note**

Some details in this case study are anonymized per company policy. 
All metrics are actual results from the project.
```

### Agency Template

```
**Client Confidentiality**

This work was completed through [Agency Name] for an enterprise client. 
Client name and specific metrics are confidential. Process and approach 
are representative of actual work delivered.
```
