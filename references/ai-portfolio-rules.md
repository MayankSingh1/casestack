# AI Portfolio Rules

Source: UXfolio, “The only 5 portfolio rules for using AI in your UX portfolio.”

These rules govern how Casestack uses AI while building a UX case study. Banned phrases in `references/writing-rules.md` are a style check. These five rules are what the case study must still prove.

Numbered as labeled. The source poster shows Rule #4 above Rule #3.

## Rule #1 — Keep your personal voice

AI can help you write, but make sure you show:

- What problem you solved
- What you learned
- Which decisions you made and why

**How Casestack applies this:** Interview for the problem, the learning, and the decision rationale in the designer’s own words. Write in that voice. Do not replace a specific answer with generic process language.

## Rule #2 — Show evidence

Back up your process descriptions with:

- Research findings
- Testing insights
- Design iterations

**How Casestack applies this:** Every process claim needs one of those three. If the designer does not have it, omit the claim or mark it unknown. Do not invent findings, test results, or iterations.

## Rule #3 — Clarify ownership

When AI supported your work, explain:

- Which tool you used and why
- What AI helped you do
- What you reviewed or changed
- Which decisions were yours

**How Casestack applies this:** Ask this only when the designer says AI supported the project. Store the answers on `aiSupport`. If AI was used, the case study states all four points. If AI was not used, omit the disclosure.

## Rule #4 — Check visuals

Review AI-generated visuals for:

- Consistent styles
- Proper alignment
- Accurate representation of your work

**How Casestack applies this:** Before shipping a page, check generated or AI-assisted images against the portfolio’s type, color, and spacing, and against the real screens, flows, or artifacts. If a visual cannot be verified as the designer’s work, use a labeled placeholder.

## Rule #5 — Show your thinking

Strong case studies still demonstrate:

- Why the problem mattered
- What evidence informed the solution
- Which trade-offs you considered
- What changed because of your work

**How Casestack applies this:** Decisions are not a list of features. Each major decision names why the problem mattered, the evidence behind the choice, the trade-off, and what changed because of the work.

## Publish bar

A case study is not ready to publish when:

- Rule #1, #2, or #5 fails
- AI was used and Rule #3 is missing
- Rule #4 fails because styles clash, alignment breaks, or a visual misrepresents the work
