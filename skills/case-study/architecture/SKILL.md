# Senior Case Study Architecture

This reference document explains each section of a senior-level portfolio case study, what hiring managers look for, and how to demonstrate seniority through your narrative.

---

## Section-by-Section Guide

### 1. Hero Section

**Purpose**: Hook the reader in 3 seconds

**Components**:
- **Title**: Project name that's memorable but clear
- **One-line outcome**: The single most impressive result (lead with impact)
- **Role**: Your specific title/function
- **Timeline**: Duration (e.g., "3 months, 2023")
- **Team**: Key collaborators and team size
- **Platform**: Web, iOS, Android, etc.

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Clear scope indication
- Outcome-first framing (not feature-first)
- Appropriate timeline for complexity
- Team context to gauge collaboration

**Example**:
```
Title: Reimagining Checkout for Global Scale
One-liner: Reduced cart abandonment by 23%, recovering $4.2M in annual revenue
Role: Lead Product Designer
Timeline: 4 months (Q2-Q3 2023)
Team: 2 designers, 5 engineers, 1 PM, 1 researcher
Platform: Web, Mobile Web
```

---

### 2. TL;DR Summary

**Purpose**: Enable quick scanning (30-second version)

**Length**: 2-4 sentences, max 80 words

**Formula**:
```
[Problem/opportunity] → [Your approach] → [Key outcome]
```

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Can you distill complexity into essence?
- Is the impact clear and credible?
- Does it make them want to read more?

**Example**:
```
Checkout abandonment was costing $18M annually due to a fragmented 
5-step flow built for US-only. I led a redesign consolidating it 
to 2 steps with smart defaults and localization for 12 markets, 
reducing abandonment by 23% and recovering $4.2M in the first year.
```

---

### 3. Context & Business Problem

**Purpose**: Set the stage—why did this project exist?

**Components**:
- Business context (company, product, market position)
- The specific problem or opportunity
- Why it mattered (stakes, urgency)
- Who cared (stakeholders, users)

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Understanding of business context (not just design tasks)
- Ability to articulate problem significance
- Awareness of stakeholder landscape

**Senior signal**: Connect design work to business outcomes

**Example**:
```
[Company] processes $2B in annual transactions across 40 countries. 
Despite strong brand recognition, conversion from cart to purchase 
lagged competitors by 15 points. Leadership prioritized checkout 
optimization as a Q2 initiative after customer research revealed 
trust and complexity as top friction points.
```

---

### 4. My Role & Collaboration

**Purpose**: Clarify your specific contribution

**Components**:
- Your title and formal responsibilities
- What you personally owned
- Who you collaborated with and how
- Scope of your influence

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Honest ownership (not inflated)
- Collaboration with cross-functional partners
- Leadership scope (did you set direction or execute?)
- Appropriate seniority for the role you're applying to

**Senior signal**: Show you influenced beyond your immediate deliverables

**Example**:
```
As Lead Product Designer, I:
- Owned end-to-end checkout UX across web and mobile
- Led a team of 2 designers, conducting weekly design critiques
- Partnered with Data Science to define success metrics
- Facilitated stakeholder alignment across 3 business units
- Collaborated daily with engineering on feasibility trade-offs
```

---

### 5. Constraints

**Purpose**: Show real-world complexity you navigated

**Types of constraints**:
- **Time**: Aggressive deadlines, competing priorities
- **Technical**: Legacy systems, platform limitations
- **Resource**: Small team, limited budget
- **Organizational**: Politics, stakeholder conflicts
- **User**: Accessibility, international, edge cases

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Pragmatism (you work within reality)
- Problem-solving under pressure
- Ability to prioritize ruthlessly

**Senior signal**: Constraints reveal how you make trade-offs

**Example**:
```
- 4-month timeline to ship before holiday peak
- Must maintain backward compatibility with legacy payment APIs
- Localization for 12 languages with no dedicated localization team
- No dedicated researcher (I conducted guerrilla testing myself)
```

---

### 6. Research & Insights

**Purpose**: Show evidence-based decision making

**Components**:
- Methods used (and why those methods)
- Sample size and participant profiles
- Key findings (3-5 max)
- Evidence (quotes, data, artifacts)

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Appropriate methods for the problem
- Rigor without over-engineering
- Ability to synthesize insights
- Connecting research to decisions

**Senior signal**: You drove research, not just consumed it

**Example**:
```
Methods:
- Analyzed 10,000 session recordings (Fullstory) to map drop-off points
- Conducted 12 user interviews across 3 markets (US, UK, Germany)
- Ran unmoderated usability tests (n=45) on prototype

Key Findings:
1. 67% abandoned at payment step—trust signals were hidden below fold
2. International users confused by US-centric address format
3. "Guest checkout" was buried, forcing unnecessary account creation

"I just want to buy this one thing. Why do I need to create an account?" 
— Participant 7, UK
```

---

### 7. Problem Framing / HMW / Success Metrics

**Purpose**: Show strategic thinking—how did you define the problem?

**Components**:
- Reframed problem statement
- How Might We questions
- Success metrics (leading and lagging)
- Targets set upfront

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Ability to reframe problems (not just accept briefs)
- Strategic metric selection
- Alignment with business goals

**Senior signal**: You defined the goalposts, not just hit them

**Example**:
```
Reframed Problem:
"Reduce checkout abandonment" → "Build trust and reduce cognitive 
load so users feel confident completing purchase in any market"

HMW:
- How might we surface trust signals at key decision points?
- How might we adapt the flow for international expectations?
- How might we make guest checkout the obvious path?

Success Metrics:
- Primary: Checkout completion rate (target: +15%)
- Secondary: Time-to-purchase (target: -30%)
- Guardrail: Support tickets related to checkout (should not increase)
```

---

### 8. Process & Key Decisions

**Purpose**: Show how you think, not just what you made

**Components**:
- Phases of work
- Key decisions made
- Alternatives considered
- Trade-offs accepted
- Rationale for choices

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Structured thinking
- Willingness to explore before committing
- Ability to articulate trade-offs
- Sound rationale

**Senior signal**: Show the decisions you DIDN'T make and why

**Example**:
```
Decision: Single-page checkout vs. multi-step wizard

Considered:
A) Single-page: All fields visible, less clicking
B) Multi-step: Progressive disclosure, less overwhelming
C) Hybrid: Collapsible sections, both visible and progressive

Chose: Hybrid (C)

Trade-off: More complex to build, but testing showed best of both 
worlds—users who scan can see everything, users who focus can step 
through. Engineering estimated +2 weeks for this approach.

Rationale: User testing showed 40% preferred scanning, 60% preferred 
stepping. Hybrid accommodated both without forcing a choice.
```

---

### 9. Solution Walkthrough

**Purpose**: Show what you built with context

**Components**:
- Solution overview (the narrative)
- Annotated visuals for key screens/flows
- Feature explanations tied to insights
- Before/after comparisons where impactful

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Clear visual communication
- Design craft and attention to detail
- Explicit connection between insight → design decision
- Understanding of the full user journey

**Senior signal**: Annotations explain WHY, not just WHAT

**Example annotation**:
```
[Screenshot of checkout flow]

1. Trust badges moved above fold (Finding: 67% abandoned at payment)
2. Smart address lookup reduces fields from 7 to 2 (Constraint: internationalization)
3. Guest checkout is default state (Finding: unnecessary account creation)
4. Order summary sticky on scroll (Testing: users wanted to verify before confirming)
```

---

### 10. Validation

**Purpose**: Show you iterated based on evidence

**Components**:
- Testing methods used
- What you learned
- What you changed
- Iteration cycles

**What hiring managers look for**:
- You test before shipping
- You respond to feedback
- You iterate (not just "ship and forget")

**Senior signal**: Show a decision that changed based on testing

**Example**:
```
Usability Testing (n=12, moderated):
- 3/12 missed the "Apply coupon" link → Made it a button
- International users expected postal code first → Reordered fields by market
- "Express checkout" label confused users → Changed to "Pay with saved info"

A/B Test (2 weeks, n=50,000):
- Variant B (trust badges above fold) outperformed by 8%
- Shipped to 100% after significance reached
```

---

### 11. Impact & Outcomes

**Purpose**: Prove your work mattered

**Components**:
- Quantified metrics (before/after)
- Business outcomes
- User outcomes
- Qualitative impacts

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Real numbers (or honest approximations)
- Connection to business value
- Appropriate attribution (not overclaiming)

**Senior signal**: Tie design work to business impact

**Example**:
```
Quantified Impact (6 months post-launch):
- Checkout completion: +23% (from 62% to 76%)
- Revenue recovered: $4.2M annually
- Time-to-purchase: -35% (from 4.5 min to 2.9 min)
- Checkout support tickets: -18%

Qualitative:
- Featured in company all-hands as Q3 highlight
- Approach adopted by 3 other product teams
- Received 4.8/5 in post-launch user survey
```

---

### 12. Reflection & Learnings

**Purpose**: Show growth mindset and self-awareness

**Components**:
- What you learned
- What you'd do differently
- Skills developed
- Advice for others

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Humility and self-awareness
- Continuous learning mindset
- Mature perspective on challenges

**Senior signal**: Honest reflection, not false modesty

**Example**:
```
Learnings:
- Localization should start day 1, not be retrofitted
- Stakeholder alignment workshops saved us 3 weeks of rework
- Guerrilla testing is better than no testing

What I'd do differently:
- Involve engineering earlier in ideation (we hit avoidable technical debt)
- Set up metrics tracking before launch (we had 2-week data gap)
```

---

### 13. Next Steps (Optional)

**Purpose**: Show forward-thinking vision

**Components**:
- Planned improvements
- Long-term vision
- Handed-off roadmap

**What hiring managers look for**:
- Strategic thinking beyond the immediate
- Understanding of product evolution

## NDA Handling Guidance

### When Under NDA

1. **Use anonymized names**: "A major fintech company" instead of company name
2. **Blur sensitive UI**: Redact numbers, names, proprietary info in screenshots
3. **Use relative metrics**: "23% improvement" instead of "$4.2M"
4. **State the constraint**: "Due to NDA, specific metrics are approximated"
5. **Focus on process**: Your thinking is yours, even if the output is confidential

### What's Usually Safe

- Your role and responsibilities
- General problem domain
- Design process and methods
- Relative outcomes (percentages, not absolute numbers)
- Sanitized mockups (with permission)

### What's Usually Restricted

- Unreleased features
- Exact revenue figures
- Proprietary user research
- Internal strategic documents
- Competitive analysis specifics

## Writing Rules

See: `references/writing-rules.md` for detailed guidance

### Quick Reference

1. **Outcome-first**: Lead with results, not process
2. **Concise**: Every sentence earns its place
3. **Scannable**: Headers, bullets, bold key points
4. **Plain language**: No jargon or buzzwords
5. **No AI slop**: Avoid hollow phrases like "streamlined," "leveraged," "utilized"
6. **Specific**: Numbers over adjectives
7. **Active voice**: "I led" not "was led by me"
8. **Honest**: Mark estimates, don't fabricate

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Feature-first, not problem-first**: Don't start with "I designed a new checkout"—start with the problem
2. **No trade-offs**: Perfect stories aren't believable
3. **Inflated ownership**: "We" when you mean "I," or "I" when you mean "we"
4. **Missing metrics**: Even relative ones matter
5. **Wall of text**: Use visuals and formatting
6. **Chronological process dump**: Focus on decisions, not timeline
7. **No research**: Even informal research counts
8. **Skipping reflection**: This shows maturity
