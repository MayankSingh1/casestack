# Case Study Writing Rules

Guidelines for writing portfolio case studies at any level that communicate effectively to hiring managers.

## Core Principles

### 1. Outcome-First

Lead with results, not process.

**Bad:**
> In this case study, I will walk through my design process for redesigning the checkout flow at Company X.

**Good:**
> Checkout abandonment was costing $18M annually. I redesigned the flow to recover $4.2M.

### 2. Concise

Every sentence earns its place. If it doesn't add information, cut it.

**Bad:**
> Throughout the course of this project, I had the opportunity to work closely with various stakeholders across multiple teams to align on a shared understanding of the problem space.

**Good:**
> I aligned 3 teams on the core problem: trust signals were hidden at checkout.

### 3. Scannable

Hiring managers skim. Make it easy.

- Use headers to chunk content
- Use bullets for lists
- Bold key metrics and findings
- Keep paragraphs under 4 sentences
- Use pull quotes for impact

### 4. Plain Language

Write at an 8th-grade reading level. Avoid jargon.

**Bad:**
> We leveraged a cross-functional ideation session to synergize on high-fidelity deliverables that would optimize the user journey touchpoints.

**Good:**
> We ran a workshop with design, engineering, and PM to brainstorm solutions. We then built detailed mockups of the top 3 ideas.

### 5. Specific Over Vague

Numbers beat adjectives. Specifics beat generalities.

**Bad:**
> The redesign significantly improved user engagement and was well received by stakeholders.

**Good:**
> Conversion increased 23%. The VP of Product featured it in the quarterly all-hands.

## Banned Phrases (AI Slop)

Banned phrases are a style check. The five portfolio rules in `references/ai-portfolio-rules.md` are separate: they are what the case study must still prove (personal voice, evidence, ownership, visual accuracy, and thinking).

These phrases are red flags that suggest the content was AI-generated or poorly written. Avoid them:

| Phrase | Why It's Bad | Alternative |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| "Leveraged" | Corporate jargon | "Used" |
| "Utilized" | Unnecessarily formal | "Used" |
| "Synergy/synergize" | Meaningless buzzword | Say what actually happened |
| "Streamlined" | Vague | Specify what improved |
| "Best-in-class" | Unsubstantiated claim | Show evidence |
| "Cutting-edge" | Cliché | Describe the innovation |
| "Seamless" | Overused, vague | Describe the specific improvement |
| "Delightful" | Subjective | Show user feedback |
| "Robust" | Vague | Be specific |
| "Holistic" | Vague | Describe what was included |
| "End-to-end" | Often meaningless | Describe actual scope |
| "Deep dive" | Cliché | "Analyzed" or "researched" |
| "Move the needle" | Vague | State the metric |
| "Low-hanging fruit" | Cliché | Say what was easy to fix |
| "Pain points" | Overused | "Problems" or "frustrations" |
| "User journey" | Can be vague | Describe the actual flow |
| "Stakeholder alignment" | Often inflated | Describe what was decided |

## Voice and Tone

### Use Active Voice

The subject does the action.

**Passive:**
> The checkout flow was redesigned by me.

**Active:**
> I redesigned the checkout flow.

### Use First Person

You did the work. Say "I."

**Distant:**
> The design team conducted user research.

**Owned:**
> I conducted 12 user interviews across 3 markets.

### Be Confident, Not Arrogant

State what you did without overstating.

**Arrogant:**
> I single-handedly saved the company $4 million.

**Confident:**
> The redesign I led recovered $4M in annual revenue.

### Be Honest About Unknowns

If you don't have a number, say so. Don't invent.

**Fabricated:**
> This resulted in a 47% increase in user satisfaction.

**Honest:**
> We saw positive feedback in user testing, though we didn't track a formal satisfaction metric.

## Sentence Structure

### Keep Sentences Short

Target under 20 words. Maximum 25.

**Too long:**
> Throughout the course of the project, we worked closely with the engineering team to understand the technical constraints while simultaneously conducting user research to validate our assumptions about user behavior.

**Better:**
> We partnered with engineering to understand technical constraints. In parallel, we ran user research to validate our assumptions.

### Vary Sentence Length

Mix short and medium for rhythm.

> The checkout flow was broken. Users abandoned at a 38% rate. I dug into session recordings, ran 12 interviews, and mapped every friction point. The root cause? Trust signals were buried below the fold.

### Start Strong

Don't bury the lead. Put the important thing first.

**Buried:**
> After conducting extensive research and multiple rounds of iteration, we finally achieved a 23% increase in conversion.

**Strong:**
> Conversion increased 23% after we moved trust signals above the fold.

## Numbers and Metrics

### Be Specific

**Vague:**
> We improved conversion significantly.

**Specific:**
> We increased conversion from 62% to 76%.

### Include Before/After

**Unclear:**
> Checkout completion reached 76%.

**Clear:**
> Checkout completion improved from 62% to 76%.

### Calculate the Delta

Do the math for the reader.

**Makes reader work:**
> Before: 62%. After: 76%.

**Easy:**
> Checkout completion: +23% (62% → 76%)

### Add Context

**Isolated:**
> We saved $4.2M.

**Contextual:**
> We recovered $4.2M annually—23% of the $18M lost to abandonment.

### Mark Estimates

If numbers aren't exact, say so.

> ~15% improvement (estimate; exact data restricted by NDA)

## Formatting

### Headers

Use H2 for main sections, H3 for subsections.

Headers should be scannable—not "Section 1" but "Research: What We Learned."

### Bullets and Lists

- Use bullets for unordered items
- Use numbers for sequences or rankings
- Keep bullets parallel in structure
- Limit to 5-7 items per list

### Bold and Italic

- **Bold** for metrics, key terms, and emphasis
- *Italic* for quotes, titles, and light emphasis
- Don't overuse—if everything is bold, nothing is

### White Space

- Separate sections clearly
- Don't cram content
- One idea per paragraph

## Checklist Before Publishing

### Content
- [ ] Outcome appears in first 100 words
- [ ] Every claim has evidence
- [ ] Metrics include before/after
- [ ] Trade-offs are explicit
- [ ] At least one user quote or data point

### Writing
- [ ] No banned phrases (grep the doc)
- [ ] Active voice throughout
- [ ] No sentence over 25 words
- [ ] Headers are descriptive
- [ ] Paragraphs under 4 sentences

### Polish
- [ ] Read aloud—does it flow?
- [ ] Cut 20%—what can go?
- [ ] One peer review completed

## Examples

### Hero One-Liner

**Bad:**
> A comprehensive redesign of the e-commerce checkout experience

**Good:**
> Reduced cart abandonment by 23%, recovering $4.2M in annual revenue

### TL;DR

**Bad:**
> In this project, I worked on redesigning the checkout flow for a large e-commerce company. We conducted user research, created wireframes, and iterated on the design based on feedback. The final design was well-received and improved key metrics.

**Good:**
> Checkout abandonment was costing $18M annually due to a 5-step flow with buried trust signals. I led a redesign that consolidated it to 2 steps with smart defaults. Result: 23% higher completion, $4.2M recovered.

### Decision with Trade-offs

**Bad:**
> We decided to use a single-page checkout after considering various options.

**Good:**
> We chose a hybrid checkout (collapsible sections) over single-page and multi-step. Trade-off: +2 weeks engineering time. Rationale: Testing showed 40% of users scan, 60% step through—hybrid served both.
